What medication causes corneal melting?
Correct answer: A. ketorolac
Rationale
Ketorolac (Acular) is a nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) used to treat postoperative inflammation and allergic conjunctivitis. Other topical NSAIDs include diclofenac sodium (Voltaren), flurbiprofen sodium (Ocufen), nepafenac (Nevanac), and bromfenac sodium (Xibrom). The NSAIDs inhibit cyclooxygenase in the metabolism of arachidonic acid. Diclofenac and ketorolac have been associated with corneal melting and perforation. Although all topical NSAIDs can theoretically cause these complications, the vast majority of cases were found with generic diclofenac, which was subsequently removed from the US market. Caution must be used in patients using these medications and especially patients with pre-existing ocular surface disease or diabetes.
Chloramphenicol is a broad-spectrum bacteriostatic antimicrobial that binds reversibly to the 50S ribosomal subunit. It can be used to treat eye infections with H influenzae, N gonorrhoeae, N meningitides, and all anaerobic bacteria. It has some activity against Klebsiella pneumoniae, Enterobacter and Serratia species, S pneumoniae, and S aureus. A major side effect is idiosyncratic aplastic anemia. Nedocromil (Alocril) and azelastine HCl (Optivar) are combination mast cell stabilizers and H1 antagonists. They are used to prevent the acute release of histamine and degranulation of mast cells in the treatment of allergic symptoms. They are not associated with corneal melting.

Q215
Fundamentals & Principles of Ophthalmology
What morphogen interacts with intracellular receptors to regulate gene expression during eye development?
Correct answer: C. retinoic acid
Rationale
External signals (morphogens) are required to guide appropriate development of embryonic eye cells. Morphogens can be divided into 2 broad groups. Group 1 ligands (retinoic acid) bind to intracellular receptors and directly affect gene expression. Group 2 ligands (fibroblast growth factor, Wnt, and sonic hedgehog) impact gene expression by binding to extracellular receptors and triggering a cascade that eventually also impacts gene expression. Importantly, cells can have a different fate depending on which morphogen they are exposed to. Also, the relative concentration of the morphogens (morphogenic gradient) impacts the differentiation of cells.

Q217
Fundamentals & Principles of Ophthalmology
What medication has been implicated in outbreaks of contamination-related infectious endophthalmitis?
Correct answer: D. bevacizumab
Rationale
Aflibercept (Eylea), bevacizumab (Avastin), pegaptanib sodium (Macugen), and ranibizumab (Lucentis) are anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (anti-VEGF) agents used to treat neovascular age-related macular degeneration, macular edema due to diabetes, and retinal vein occlusion, and other eye conditions. Aflibercept, pegaptanib sodium, and ranibizumab are sterilely packaged in single-use containers and are approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for several ophthalmic indications. In contrast, bevacizumab, which is molecularly related to ranibizumab, is not FDA approved for ophthalmic indications. However, it is routinely packaged at compounding pharmacies and administered intravitreally for ophthalmic conditions because it costs less than any of the FDA-approved anti-VEGF agents.
Administering a medication for indications other than those for which it was approved by the FDA is referred to as "off label" use. In 2011, outbreaks of endophthalmitis related to compounded bevacizumab led the American Academy of Ophthalmology to issue new guidelines to help ensure the safety and sterility of compounded medications. Understanding which medications are compounded and which are used off-label is important for clinicians in guiding informed consent and patient-care related discussions.

Q218
Fundamentals & Principles of Ophthalmology
Where does cranial nerve III (CN III) typically separate into superior and inferior divisions?
Correct answer: D. within the superior orbital fissure
Rationale
Cranial nerve III (CN III) usually separates into superior and inferior divisions after passing through the annulus of Zinn in the orbit. Both the superior and inferior divisions pass through the superior orbital fissure within the annulus. However, in some individuals, CN III divides within the anterior cavernous sinus. The optic canal carries the optic nerve through the annulus of Zinn. CN III maintains a topographic organization even in the midbrain, so lesions almost anywhere along its course may cause a divisional nerve palsy. The superior division of CN III innervates the superior rectus and levator palpebrae muscles. The larger inferior division splits into 3 branches to supply the medial rectus, inferior rectus, and inferior oblique muscles. Parasympathetic pupillary fibers also course along the inferior division of CN III to enter the orbit and synapse with the ciliary ganglion.

Q219
Fundamentals & Principles of Ophthalmology
The parasympathetic fibers travel with what nerve fibers to reach the ciliary ganglion?
Correct answer: A. the inferior division of the third cranial nerve (CN III)
Rationale
Parasympathetic fibers that travel with CN III reach the ciliary ganglion via the inferior division of CN III when it splits into superior and inferior divisions. The ciliary ganglion is located in the orbit, situated on the lateral surface of the ophthalmic artery lateral to the optic nerve and medial to the lateral rectus muscle, approximately 1 cm in front of the orbital apex. Parasympathetic fibers destined to synapse in the ciliary ganglion travel with CN III. When CN III splits into superior and inferior divisions, the parasympathetics travel with the inferior division to reach the ciliary ganglion. Sympathetic fibers pass through the ganglion, but do not synapse there.

Which extraocular muscle originates from the annulus of Zinn?
Correct answer: D. lateral rectus
Rationale
The annulus of Zinn consists of superior and inferior orbital tendons and is the origin of the 4 rectus muscles. Q221
Fundamentals & Principles of Ophthalmology
Soft contact lenses with low oxygen permeability cause corneal edema via the accumulation of what substance?
Correct answer: B. lactic acid
Rationale
Under anaerobic (low-oxygen) conditions, glucose, the main metabolic substrate for the cornea, is converted to pyruvic acid and then to lactic acid. Lactic acid accumulation in the stroma increases the osmotic load, which draws water into the cornea and causes corneal edema.
Carbon dioxide, aldehyde dehydrogenase, and proteoglycans do not accumulate in the cornea in hypoxic conditions. Carbon dioxide is a product of glucose metabolism via glycolysis under aerobic conditions. Aldehyde dehydrogenase is a soluble protein found in the cornea that absorbs ultraviolet B (UVB) light. Proteoglycans confer hydrophilic properties to the corneal stroma and interact with collagen fibrils to provide corneal clarity.

Q223
Fundamentals & Principles of Ophthalmology
Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) block prostaglandin biosynthesis by inhibition of what enzyme?
Correct answer: B. cyclo-oxygenase
Rationale
The production of eicosanoids, which include prostaglandins, leukotrienes, prostacyclin, and thromboxane, is important to understand. Prostaglandins (PGs) are especially relevant to the eye, as they impact aqueous humor dynamics, affect the blood-ocular barrier, and can cause miosis. This important cascade starts with the release of arachidonic acid from membrane phospholipids by phospholipase A2, an enzyme blocked by corticosteroids. Freed arachidonic acid can then be converted to prostaglandins by cyclooxygenase-1 (COX-1) or -2 (COX-2). Thromboxane synthetase acts further "downstream," converting primary PGs to thromboxane, which is a potent vasoconstrictor and platelet-aggregating agent. Alternative to PG synthesis, lipoxygenase can convert free arachidonic acid to leukotrienes. Topical and systemic NSAIDs block prostaglandin (PG) synthesis by the irreversible binding of cyclooxygenase (COX).

What feature of the retinal pigment epithelium contributes to the blood-retina barrier?
Correct answer: D. lateral tight junctions
Rationale
The retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) has multiple structural specializations that underlie its support of the function and health of the neurosensory retina. Tight junctions located in the lateral surfaces of adjacent RPE form the outer blood-retina barrier. The differing apical versus basal distributions of ion channels and transporters support the dehydrated state of the subretinal space. The envelopment of the photoreceptor outer segments by the RPE apical microvilli facilitates the phagocytosis of shed discs as well as the renewal of visual pigments needed for the visual cycle. Melanosomes absorb light.

Q232
Fundamentals & Principles of Ophthalmology
What abnormality of embryologic development is responsible for the formation of optic nerve colobomas?
Correct answer: B. incomplete closure of the embryonic fissure
Rationale
Optic nerve coloboma results from defective closure of the posterior embryonic or choroidal fissure. It occurs inferonasally in the optic nerve head and is associated with colobomatous defects of other tissues along the embryonic fissure, including retina and choroid, ciliary body, and iris.

Failure of neural crest differentiation profoundly affects development of the ocular structures and results in cryptophthalmos (absence of the eyelids, conjunctiva, cornea, and lens). The primary vitreous consists of fibrillar material, mesenchymal cells, and vascular components.

The tunica vasculosa lentis surrounds the developing lens, the vasa hyaloidea propria is a network of vessels posterior to the lens, and the hyaloid artery connects the vasa hyaloidea propria to the optic nerve. Failure of these vessels to regress results in persistent fetal vasculature and often microphthalmia. Early in embryogenesis, the optic vesicle influences the specific region of head ectoderm to form the lens placode. The lens placode later invaginates to become the lens vesicle. If there is failure of induction of the lens placode, the lens never develops. This is primary congenital aphakia and is associated with mutations in PAX6. Other severe ocular and systemic abnormalities also occur.

The adverse effects of reactive oxygen species (ROS) have been repeatedly proposed as causal factors in what ocular disease?
Correct answer: B. cataract
Rationale
The adverse effects of ROS have been repeatedly proposed as causal factors in many vision-threatening diseases, including cataract, age-related macular degeneration (AMD), diabetic retinopathy, and glaucoma. Lipid peroxides are formed when ROS react with unsaturated fatty acids. The oxidation of membrane phospholipids has been hypothesized to increase the permeability of cell membranes, which contributes eventually to cell malfunction and potentially to cell lysis.

What geographic zone of the conjunctiva adheres firmly to the tarsus?
Correct answer: A. palpebral
Rationale
The conjunctiva can be divided into 3 geographic zones: palpebral, forniceal, and bulbar. The palpebral conjunctiva begins at the mucocutaneous junction of the eyelid and covers the lid's inner surface This part adheres firmly to the tarsus. The forniceal conjunctiva is the zone that is freely movable and redundant in the fornices. It becomes enmeshed with fibrous elements of the levator aponeurosis and Müller muscle in the upper eyelid and with fibrous expansions of the inferior rectus muscle sheath, which fuse with the inferior tarsal muscle in the lower lid. The bulbar conjunctiva fuses with the Tenon capsule and inserts into the limbus. There is no superficial zone of conjunctiva.

In what vitreous-associated process has age-related loss of type IX collagen been implicated?
Correct answer: A. vitreous liquefaction
Rationale
Vitreous liquefaction, also known as syneresis, begins with the breakdown of collagen fibrils into smaller fragments. This liquefaction is thought to occur because of a loss of shielding of type II collagen by type IX collagen. This process has no direct effect on the development of vitreous hemorrhage unless it leads to the development of posterior vitreous detachment (PVD). A PVD can protect against retinal neovascularization by eliminating the scaffold for fibrovascular proliferation. Oxygen tension increases in the posterior chamber in postvitrectomized eyes.

Q237
Fundamentals & Principles of Ophthalmology
What is the physiologic rate of endothelial cell loss with normal aging?
Correct answer: D. 0.6% per year
Rationale
The corneal endothelium, located posterior to the Descemet membrane, is a monolayer of hexagonal cells with a diameter of 20 µm. In young adults, the normal endothelial cell count is approximately 3000/mm2 centrally. The number of endothelial cells is higher in the periphery and decreases with age. There is a concomitant spreading and thinning of the remaining cells. The rate of physiologic corneal endothelial cell loss with normal aging has been reported to be 0.6% per year.

Q239
Fundamentals & Principles of Ophthalmology
The disruption of what cornea layer in laser subepithelial keratomileusis (LASEK) is thought to be the cause of increased risk for visual complications postoperatively due to central cornea haze?
Correct answer: D. Bowman layer
Rationale
It is thought that the Bowman layer, by virtue of its acellularity and packing distribution, serves to prevent exposure of stromal corneal keratocytes to growth factors secreted by corneal epithelial cells. This effect is notable because during photorefractive keratectomy (PRK) or LASEK, the Bowman layer, along with the anterior corneal stromal tissue, is removed. In these procedures, corneal haze is a potentially significant postoperative visual complication, presumably because the stromal keratocytes are exposed to regenerating epithelial growth factors and take on fibroblastic behaviors. On the other hand, in laser in situ keratomileusis (LASIK), the Bowman layer is transected but still retained, and thus central cornea haze is extremely rare.

What part of the corneal epithelium can cause recurrent corneal erosions if it does not re-form correctly after trauma?
Correct answer: D. hemidesmosomes
Rationale
The anterior surface of the cornea is covered by a lipophilic, nonkeratinized, stratified squamous epithelium. The basal cell layer is attached to the basal lamina by hemidesmosomes. Q241
Fundamentals & Principles of Ophthalmology
What orbital surgical space contains the ciliary ganglion?
Correct answer: D. intraconal
Rationale
The orbit comprises 5 surgical spaces: subperiosteal, sub-Tenon, extraconal, intraconal, and subarachnoid.
The ciliary ganglion is found on the lateral surface of the optic nerve sheath, 1 centimeter anterior to the annulus of Zinn, in the orbital apex between the optic nerve and the lateral rectus muscle. Therefore, it is intraconal.
The only structures found in the subarachnoid space of the orbit are the optic nerve and the central retinal artery/vein.
Both the subperiosteal and sub-Tenon spaces contain no significant structures, although a variety of vessels pierce and pass through them. Both of these locations represent potential dead spaces where fluid may collect because of the loose adherence of the linings to the underlying anatomy (periosteum to bone and Tenon capsule to sclera), and they are relatively avascular.

Q246
Fundamentals & Principles of Ophthalmology
In the normal physiologic human eye, what component of the aqueous is always higher in concentration in the aqueous than in plasma?
Correct answer: D. lactate
Rationale
Aqueous is the major nutrient source for the avascular lens and cornea and provides a route for the removal of waste products. Ocular fluids are separated from blood by barriers formed by tight junctions between epithelial and endothelial cells. This makes up the blood-aqueous barrier. Aqueous enters the posterior chamber from the ciliary processes by means of active and passive physiologic mechanisms, resulting in specific concentrations of various ions to maintain a specific composition of the fluid. In order of highest to lowest concentration, the components of aqueous humor are (see Table 9-1):
  • Na+ (163 mmol/kg H2O)
  • Cl- (134 mmol/kg H2O)
  • HCO-3 (20 mmol/kg H2O)
  • Glucose (3 mmol/kg H2O)
  • Ascorbate (1.06 mmol/kg H2O).
Lactate is the most abundant organic anion in the aqueous, and its concentration in the aqueous is always higher than that in plasma. Calcium is generally 50% less concentrated in the aqueous versus plasma, and aqueous concentration of glucose is about 70% of the concentration found in plasma (can be higher in diabetics, leading to problems with refraction due to lens retention of glucose short-term, and cataract formation long-term). Urea's concentration in the aqueous is about 80%-90% of that in plasma.

What type of fracture is more likely to occur on the orbital floor prior to puberty?
Correct answer: A. trapdoor-type fracture
Rationale
Trapdoor-type, stress, greenstick, and Salter-Harris fractures can all occur during childhood and puberty. All but the stress fractures are almost exclusive to children and teens. These fractures can occur in any long bone, from fingers and toes, to arm and leg bones.

Q384

The retinal pigment epithelium is derived from what embryonic layer?
Correct answer: D. neuroectoderm, outer layer of the optic cup
Rationale
Embryology can be difficult to retain but also helpful in understanding the basis of abnormalities in the formed eye. In the first 2 weeks after fertilization, the zygote divides to form a 16-cell morula. The morula then forms a cystic blastula. The blastula contains a small inner mass of cells called the embryoblast, which will become the embryo. The cells that line the cystic portion of the blastula form the trophoblast, which will become the placenta and support tissues. The embryoblast divides into 2 layers of cells, the epiblast and hypoblast. The majority of the epiblast will become ectoderm, but some of the cells migrate in between the epiblast and hypoblast layers to form mesoderm. These mesenchymal cells form the notochord, a primitive axial skeleton. The notochord induces the overlying ectoderm to differentiate into neuroectoderm (neural plate). The brain and eye develop from the most anterior portion of the neural plate.
The neural plate begins to fold inward. The cells at the apex of these neural folds become the neural crest cells. The neural folds close to form a neural tube with overlying surface ectoderm. As the neural tube closes, optic pits develop from the anterior portion (now the forebrain vesicle). The optic pits deepen and become the optic vesicles. As the optic vesicle approaches the outer wall of the embryo, there is a focal thickening of the surface ectoderm (the lens placode). The lens placode invaginates to form the lens vesicle. Simultaneously, the optic vesicle invaginates within itself to form a 2-layered optic cup. The outer layer of the optic cup will become a monolayer of retinal pigment epithelium. The inner invaginated layer of the optic cup will become neurosensory retina.

Alterations in glucose metabolism can result in cataract formation. Under normal physiologic conditions, what is the primary pathway by which glucose is converted into adenosine triphosphate (ATP) in the human lens?
Correct answer: B. glycolysis
Rationale

The polyol pathway (also called sorbitol-aldose reductase pathway) becomes important in the setting of hyperglycemia, in which glucose is metabolized through the polyol pathway and sorbitol is generated. Sorbitol does not readily traverse cell membranes, and its accumulation is thought to play a role in the development of sugar cataracts.
