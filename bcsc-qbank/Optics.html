Q422
Clinical Optics & Vision Rehabilitation
In addition to greater durability and easier maintenance, what is an advantage of rigid gas permeable lenses over soft contact lenses?
Correct answer: D. irregular astigmatism correction
Rationale
Unlike soft contact lenses, which conform to the shape of the cornea, rigid gas permeable (RGP) lenses are able to correct for irregular astigmatism because the rigid shape of the lens allows a tear lens to form behind it and "fill in" the corneal irregularities to improve the refractive surface.
RGP lenses are more expensive but tend to be more durable with simpler care than soft contact lenses. RGP lenses require a longer period of adaptation, are more uncomfortable, and are more difficult to fit than commercial soft contact lenses.

Q423
Clinical Optics & Vision Rehabilitation
What laser-tissue interaction is the basis of the effect of the excimer laser used in laser in situ keratomileusis (LASIK)?
Correct answer: D. photoablation
Rationale
Laser in situ keratomileusis (LASIK) and photorefractive keratectomy (PRK), common refractive surgeries of the cornea, are based on photoablation using excimer lasers that generate photons with wavelengths in the ultraviolet (UV) range that are absorbed within the corneal tissue.
Photocoagulation, which is associated with protein and collagen denaturation, is the most commonly used thermal laser–tissue interaction in ophthalmic surgery. The most common ophthalmic applications of photocoagulation include laser coagulation to prevent retinal detachments, photocoagulation of retinal vessel disease and panretinal photocoagulation in diabetic retinopathy.
The most important ophthalmic application of a photodisruptive interaction is posterior capsulotomy using the Nd:YAG laser. During a photodisruption procedure, it is the mechanical (acoustic) wave and not the laser light itself (as with plasma-induced ablation) that breaks the capsule.
Photochemical interaction, sometimes referred to as photoactivation, takes place at long exposure times, ranging from seconds to continuously, and very low power densities or irradiances (typically 1 W/cm2). This type of interaction is based on the use of a photo-sensitizing dye (eg, rose bengal, riboflavin, or verteporfin) that serves as a chemical (electron reaction) catalyst. Laser irradiation, at a wavelength coupled to the specific dye used, causes a photochemical reaction only within tissues where the dye is present and when irradiated.
Plasma-induced ablation is the principle upon which femtosecond lasers are based.

A 3 PD base-in and a 4 PD base-up prism can be combined into a single prism. What would be the power of the combined prism?
Correct answer: B. 5 PD
Rationale
When combining multiple prisms, the vector and axis are calculated by using right-angle trigonometry (the Pythagorean theorem (a^2+b^2=c^2)). In this case, one vector would be 3 in the "x-axis" and one vector would be 4 in the "y-axis." This would create a right triangle with a resultant vector that is 5 (PD). The axis is calculated by the formula (theta = cos^-1 (a/c), where a = adjacent side, c=hypotenuse). The opposite and adjacent sides of the triangle are 4 and 3. Therefore, the hypotenuse is 5 (4^2+3^2 = 25 = c^2, c = 5). Then, the axis is the cos^-1(3/5) = 53 degrees.

While performing streak retinoscopy, the examiner notes that the light reflex has an asymmetric "scissor" shape. What is the most likely explanation?
Correct answer: B. irregular astigmatism
Rationale
"Scissoring" (asymmetric distortion of the upper and lower aspects of the streak) most commonly occurs when there is a significant disparity in power between the upper cornea and the lower cornea. This is also a description of irregular astigmatism, which is often seen in patients with keratoconus. High myopia, hyperopia, and regular astigmatism will alter the "with" and "against" behavior of the reflex, but are not responsible for the scissors reflex.

LogMAR notation (base-10 logarithm of the minimum angle of resolution) is frequently used to record visual acuity in clinical trials. What is the difference in LogMAR scores associated with a doubling of the minimum angle of resolution (such as the difference between a visual acuity of 20/40 and 20/80 or between 6/12 and 6/24)?
Correct answer: C. 0.3 LogMAR
Rationale
A change in acuity by a factor of 10 (such as a change from 20/20 to 20/200) corresponds to a difference of 1.0 LogMAR, since 1.0 is the common logarithm of 10. The common logarithm of 2 is 0.301, which we round to 0.30 for clinical purposes. This is “built-in” to the common Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) eye charts, which feature proportional steps of 0.10 LogMAR between lines, so that a 3-line change in acuity corresponds to a change of 0.30 LogMAR (ie, a doubling [or halving] of the visual angle).

Q433
Clinical Optics & Vision Rehabilitation
In a phakic eye, at what range of wavelengths is the retina susceptible to photochemical injury?
Correct answer: C. 400 nm-550 nm
Rationale
The cornea, lens, and retina are susceptible to injury at various wavelengths of light.
The cornea is susceptible to almost all wavelengths of light: UV light (180-400 nm) can cause photokeratitis, and visible and infrared light (400 nm-1 mm) can cause thermal injury.
The lens is susceptible to injury from UV light (180-400 nm), causing cataracts, as well as to thermal injury from light wavelengths 400-3000 nm.
The retina is susceptible to thermal injury from 400-1400 nm, and photochemical injury from blue light (400-550 nm, 310-550 nm for an aphakic eye). The susceptibility to damage from blue light is the basis for incorporating UV-blocking and blue-blocking chromophores in some intraocular lenses (IOLs).

Q434
Clinical Optics & Vision Rehabilitation
What adjustment should be made to the IOL power selection in an eye containing silicone oil?
Correct answer: C. Increase IOL power by 3-5 D if the silicone oil will remain in the eye.
Rationale
Silicone oil can make IOL selection difficult for 2 reasons: obtaining measurements and selecting lenses. IOL measurements with an ultrasonic biometer can be unreliable, given the different ultrasound velocity for silicone oil (980 m/s) and vitreous (1532 m/s). Optical methods such as the IOLMaster are much more reliable.
If the silicone oil is to be removed from the eye in the near future, one can proceed with lens selection as usual. However, if the silicone oil is to remain in the eye, adjustments must be made. Silicone oil creates a negative lens power in the eye when coupled with a biconvex IOL, which is standard in the US. Most surgeons elect to increase IOL power by 3-5 diopters in order to avoid a hyperopic surprise. Of note, silicone lenses should not be used with silicone oil in the eye.

Q435
Clinical Optics & Vision Rehabilitation
A Galilean telescope is made with a –12.00 D eyepiece and a +6.00 D objective. What is the angular magnification of the telescope?
Correct answer: B. 2×
Rationale
Angular magnification (MA) of a telescope is equal to the power of the eyepiece (FE) divided by the power of the objective (FO). MA = (-) FE / FO = (-) -12 / +6 = 2×  Galilean telescopes have a weak (+) objective lens with a strong (-) eyepiece lens. The lenses are separated by the difference of their focal lengths. An upright image is formed.
Galilean telescopes are usually 2× magnification. Surgical loupes and low vision aides are examples of Galilean telescopes. They are useful for low vision work because the image is upright, the device is short, inexpensive, weighs less, and has a large exit pupil, making centering easy.
The Keplerian telescope has a weak (+) objective lens and a strong (+) eyepiece lens. The lenses are separated by the sum of their focal lengths. An inverted image is formed so they need an erecting lens or prism. Keplerian telescopes are usually 4× magnification or stronger. An example of a Keplerian telescope is the astronomical telescope.

Q436
Clinical Optics & Vision Rehabilitation
What combination of optical elements is required to construct a Keplerian telescope that produces an upright image?
Correct answer: B. a lower-power (+) lens and a higher-power (+) lens, separated by the sum of their focal lengths, with an inverting prism
Rationale
A lower-power (+) lens and a higher-power (+) lens, separated by the sum of their focal lengths, produces a Keplerian telescope. A Keplerian telescope produces an inverted image; therefore, inverting prisms are required to make an upright image. Most binoculars are made from Keplerian telescopes with inverting prisms.
A lower-power (+) lens and a higher-power (-) lens, separated by the difference in their focal lengths, produces a Galilean telescope. A Galilean telescope produces an upright image; therefore, no inverting prism is required.

Correct answer: D. The red light will diverge more than the green light.
Rationale
Diffraction describes the divergence of light as it traverses through an aperture. The smaller the aperture is relative to the wavelength of light, the more pronounced the diffraction. Q440
Clinical Optics & Vision Rehabilitation
While surfing, a person spots a shark nearby in the water. Where is the shark, in reality, compared with where the person sees its image from the surfboard?
Correct answer: D. closer to the person
Rationale
If light travels from a denser medium (in this case, water) to a less dense medium (in this case, air), then light rays bend away from the surface normal (according to Snell's law). This redirection of light produces image displacement, thus causing the shark to appear further away than it actually is (therefore, it actually is closer than it appears).
However, for a shark (in the water) looking up at a surfer (in the air), the surfer would actually be further from the shark than the surfer appeared.

Q441
Clinical Optics & Vision Rehabilitation
Why are daily-wear contact lenses favored over extended-wear lenses in the United States?
Correct answer: C. increased risk of keratitis with extended-wear lenses
Rationale
Daily-wear lenses have been favored in the US ever since reports in the 1980s showed increased incidence of keratitis with extended-wear lenses.
Additional risk factors for extended wear complications include previous eye infections, lens use while swimming, exposure to smoke, dry eye, eyelid margin disease, and allergy. Daily-wear users are exposed to the same risks, only less so.
About 10% of contact lenses fitted in the US are rigid gas-permeable (RGP) lenses. The main advantages of RGP lenses are the high quality of vision they offer, including the correction of corneal astigmatism, as well as durability and simplicity of lens care. The main disadvantages of RBP contact lenses are initial discomfort, longer period of adaptation, greater knowledge and effort required for fitting, and the greater cost of replacing a lost or damaged lens.

Q443
Clinical Optics & Vision Rehabilitation
What is the effect of common refractive errors on the apparent size of the optic disc as seen with a direct ophthalmoscope?
Correct answer: D. The optic disc will appear smaller in an aphakic eye than an emmetropic eye.
Rationale
With direct ophthalmoscopy, the examiner uses the optics of the patient’s eye as a simple magnifier to look at the retina. The optics of the normal eye are approximately +60 D, so using the formula for a simple magnifier, the magnification is 60/4, or 15×. If the patient’s eye is myopic, a minus lens is dialed in, to overcome the extra plus power “error lens” inside the patient’s eye. Those 2 lenses create a Galilean telescope effect, increasing magnification and decreasing the field of view. Similarly, the retina of a hyperopic eye will be magnified less than 15× because of the reverse Galilean telescope created by the minus power error lens inside the patient’s eye and the plus lens of the direct ophthalmoscope. Thus, the size of the optic disc will appear smaller in a hyperopic eye—of which aphakia is an extreme example—than in an emmetropic eye or a myopic eye.

Q450
Clinical Optics & Vision Rehabilitation
What part of an intraocular lens (IOL) is typically responsible for a patient experiencing glare?
Correct answer: D. edge
Rationale
The presence of IOLs may cause numerous optical phenomena. Various light-related visual phenomena encountered by pseudophakic patients are termed dysphotopsias. These phenomena are divided into positive and negative dysphotopsias. Positive dysphotopsias are characterized by brightness, streaks, and rays emanating from a central point source of light, sometimes with a diffuse, hazy glare. Such optical phenomena may be related to light reflection and refraction along the edges of the IOL. High-index acrylic lenses with square or truncated edges produce a more intense edge glare. These phenomena may also be due to internal re-reflection within the IOL itself; such reflection is more likely to occur with materials that have a higher refractive index, such as acrylic. With a less steeply curved anterior surface, the lens may be more likely to have internal reflections that are directed toward the fovea and are therefore more distracting.
Multifocal optics can cause image degradation, "ghost" images, decreased contrast sensitivity, and reduced performance in low light. They do not tend to produce glare.

What is the definition of n, the refractive index?
Correct answer: C. speed of light in a vacuum / speed of light in a medium
Rationale
The speed of light in a vacuum is one of the fundamental constants of nature. The speed of light in air is essentially the same. It is convenient to refer to the speed of light in other materials by comparison to the speed of light in a vacuum: the ratio of the speed of light in a vacuum to the speed of light in another medium is referred to as the refractive index of the medium. Q530
Clinical Optics & Vision Rehabilitation
Corneal haze secondary to corneal edema is primarily caused by what light phenomenon?
Correct answer: A. scattering
Rationale
Light scattering occurs when small particles suspended in a transparent medium interfere with the transmittance of light and cause photons to deviate from a straight path. Short wavelengths of light are scattered more strongly than are longer wavelengths. Larger particles scatter light more intensely than do smaller particles. In a healthy cornea, the tightly arranged and regularly spaced collagen molecules minimize the effects of scattering. When a cornea becomes edematous, excess fluid in the stroma disrupts the regular collagen structure, causing light scattering.
Diffraction may be thought of as light, when encountering an obstacle, scattering off in different directions at the edges. What really happens is that light interacts with the electrons in the material, most noticeably with the electrons at the edges. Once light scatters off in different directions, it usually interferes with other parts of the diffracted light. Hence, in classical wave theory, the phenomenon of diffraction of light, as it passes beyond an obstacle of comparable size to its wavelength, is generally described as interference effects of the emerging light.

Q564
Clinical Optics & Vision Rehabilitation
What is a property of all ophthalmic lasers?
Correct answer: D. stimulated emission
Rationale
Laser light is created when atoms of an active medium are exposed to a source of energy (the pumping source). This introduction of energy causes most of the active medium’s electrons to rise to a higher energy state, a condition called population inversion. Some of these high-energy electrons undergo spontaneous emission, generating photons. If these photons first encounter low-energy electrons, they are merely absorbed, but if they encounter other high-energy electrons, stimulated emission occurs. To maintain the chain reaction of stimulated emissions, mirrors are placed at each end of the cavity, an optical feedback arrangement. One mirror reflects totally and the other partially. Most of the coherent light generated is reflected back into the cavity to produce more stimulated emissions. The relatively small amount of light that is allowed to pass through the partially reflecting mirror produces the actual laser beam.
The active medium or laser cavity can contain a gas, a liquid, or a solid (often a semiconductor) or other substance capable of allowing stimulated emission. Plasmas represent a highly energized gaseous states in which electrons and positively charged ions (nuclei) are not associated. A plasma state cannot exist in a solid or liquid.
The coupling between the amount of energy added to the cavity compared to the laser energy emitted, or efficiency, can vary among materials and cavity characteristics.
Lasers can be continuous wave or pulsed, depending on the pumping source.

Temporal dragging of the fovea in retinopathy of prematurity results in pseudoexotropia. This effect between the pupillary and visual axis is best described as what?
Correct answer: B. positive angle kappa
Rationale
Involution of abnormal retinal vessels in retinopathy of prematurity (ROP) Is associated with several complications, including a peripheral lattice-like degeneration, failure of peripheral vascularization, and abnormal tortuosity of vasculature. Angle kappa refers to the angle between the anatomic pupillary axis and the visual axis (Figure). Angle alpha refers to the angle between the visual axis and the optical axis. Angle alpha is positive when the visual axis in object space is nasal to the optical axis.

A child has a cycloplegic refraction OD +6.00 D, OS +2.00 D. What is the best way to manage the anisometropia?
Correct answer: B. full correction
Rationale
In children still developing binocularity, a little anisometropia can lead to a large amblyopia. Anisometropic amblyopia is a fairly common entity and easily missed because the strabismic cosmetic defect is often absent. Full correction of the anisometropia might lead to improvement of visual acuity. Treatment of amblyopia after full correction is total occlusion of the better eye. Better yet, amblyopia should be prevented by providing the best optical focus for each eye and the best potential for binocular vision. The younger the child, the more likely that full correction will prevent amblyopia. In large anisometropia, particularly in older children, contact lenses are necessary to overcome the large image-size difference. Pleoptics has no place in the treatment of anisometropia per se.

How does an intracorneal ring segment change the corneal surface?
Correct answer: A. It displaces lamellar bundles and shortens the corneal arc length.
Rationale
The correct answer is displacement of lamellar bundles with flattening of central cornea. Corneal ring segments are FDA approved to treat mild levels of myopia (-1.00 to -3.00 D). What logMAR value is equivalent to a Snellen visual acuity of 20/20?
Correct answer: C. 0.00
Rationale
LogMAR is calculated by taking the logarithm of the reciprocal of the Snellen fraction. For instance, if the Snellen visual acuity is 20/200, then the reciprocal is 200/20, or 10, and the logarithm of 10 is 1. Likewise, for a 20/20 eye, the reciprocal of the Snellen visual acuity is also 20/20, or 1, and the logarithm of 1 is 0.
